Areeb Ahmad

I work on mechanistic interpretability: working out how language models compute, one component and one direction at a time. Most of my work uses causal interventions, and asks what those interventions actually measure. The motivation is AI safety. If we are going to audit or steer models through their internals, the tools we read them with need to be calibrated.

Research intern, Lexsi Labs, on alignment and interpretability. Before that, the Exploration Lab at IIT Kanpur with Ashutosh Modi.

Questions I work on

  • What does an ablation measure?

    Conventional ablations are uncalibrated points on a single dose axis. Read that way, apparent self-repair is a fixed gain that can be predicted from the weights.

  • What is the right unit of analysis?

    Attention heads and MLPs are not atomic. Decomposed into singular directions, a head that looks like it does one thing can carry several overlapping subfunctions.

  • When does a model commit to an answer, and how sure is it?

    Confidence is recalibrated in the upper layers after the answer is already settled, and a low-dimensional direction in the residual stream controls it. Path patching shows where a commonsense decision starts and which later layers reinforce it.

Papers

  1. Preprint, 2026

    Every Ablation Is a Dose: Counterweights and the Semblance of Self-Repair

    Every ablation is a point on a dose axis λ. A downstream unit's response is affine in λ, and its slope is a gain that exists before any ablation. What looks like self-repair is a counterweight doing its usual job.

    Areeb Ahmad, Pratinav Seth, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu

  2. NeurIPS 2025

    Beyond Components: Singular Vector-Based Interpretability of Transformer Circuits

    Decompose a head or MLP into its singular directions and the computation splits apart: a single functional head carries several overlapping subfunctions, each aligned with a different direction.

    Areeb Ahmad*, Abhinav Joshi*, Ashutosh Modi

  3. EMNLP 2025

    Calibration Across Layers: Understanding Calibration Evolution in LLMs

    Tracked layer by layer, a model's confidence overshoots after its answer is settled, then is recalibrated in the upper layers. A low-dimensional residual-stream direction improves calibration without touching accuracy.

    Abhinav Joshi, Areeb Ahmad, Ashutosh Modi

  4. NAACL 2025

    Towards Quantifying Commonsense Reasoning with Mechanistic Insights

    Graphs of everyday activities generate enormous question sets for commonsense reasoning. Path patching with conjugate prompts locates the decision: it starts around layer 20 and is reinforced by the layers after it.

    Abhinav Joshi, Areeb Ahmad, Divyaksh Shukla, Ashutosh Modi

  5. NeurIPS 2024

    COLD: Causal Reasoning in Closed Daily Activities

    From scripts of daily activities, build an observational graph and a causal graph, then ask models to tell a cause from an event that merely comes later. Queried exhaustively, they are still far from reliable.

    Abhinav Joshi*, Areeb Ahmad*, Ashutosh Modi

  6. IJCAI 2023

    ScriptWorld: Text Based Environment for Learning Procedural Knowledge

    A text environment built from crowd-written scripts of everyday tasks. At each step the agent picks the next action from a few options, so procedural knowledge is what it has to learn.

    Abhinav Joshi, Areeb Ahmad, Umang Pandey, Ashutosh Modi

Foraging with deep reinforcement learning

An undergraduate project from 2021 that I'm still fond of. When should a forager leave a patch that is running out and go looking for a better one? I framed the problem from behavioural ecology as a Markov decision process in a 20k×20k environment and trained a Double DQN agent on it.

The agent scored 21% higher on average than the 183 people who played the same game, and its patch-leaving behaviour matched what foraging theory predicts for animals. Supervised by Ashutosh Modi and Arjun Ramakrishnan.
